# TTT Closed Universe — Falsification

> **Purpose:** Define in advance how the TTT-Closed-Universe hypothesis could fail.
>
> The objective of this document is not to defend TTT, but to prevent the theory from becoming unfalsifiable.

---

## 1. Scientific Principle

TTT-Closed-Universe proposes a substantially different cosmological interpretation from standard Big Bang / general-relativistic cosmology.

Therefore the appropriate question is not:

> "Can TTT explain this observation somehow?"

The appropriate question is:

> "What observation or mathematical result would force us to reject this version of TTT?"

A theory that can accommodate every possible result has no effective falsification criterion.

---

## 2. Falsification Level 1 — Mathematical Consistency

TTT must first survive its own definitions.

### F1.1 Undefined Operations

If a central operation such as

\[
X\odot Y
\]

cannot be defined consistently over the claimed domain, the corresponding axiom must be rejected or rewritten.

### F1.2 Contradictory Axioms

If the stated axioms imply both

\[
XYZ=-1
\]

and

\[
XYZ=+1
\]

under the same conditions, the axiom system is inconsistent.

### F1.3 Non-Closure

If the claimed algebraic structure does not close under the operations used by the theory, the corresponding structural claim fails.

---

## 3. Falsification Level 2 — Geometric Model

### F2.1 Tetrahedral Equilibrium

The exact identity

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{4}\vec v_i=0
\]

for a regular tetrahedron is not itself falsifiable; it is mathematics.

What can be falsified is the claim that this structure is the correct physical model of a proposed system.

If a proposed physical mapping requires non-tetrahedral behavior that cannot be represented within the model, that application fails.

### F2.2 Cl(3) Correspondence

The \(\mathrm{Cl}(3)\) correspondence fails if an allegedly independent physical degree of freedom cannot be represented without adding a genuinely new algebraic basis element.

The test is:

1. define the observable;
2. determine its transformation properties;
3. determine whether it is represented by an existing scalar/vector/bivector/pseudoscalar component;
4. if not, determine the required extension.

The conclusion must be recorded even when it disproves the original hypothesis.

---

## 4. Falsification Level 3 — Closed Cosmology

### F3.1 No Consistent Closed Solution

If no nonsingular closed cosmological solution can simultaneously reproduce the basic observed universe, the central closed-universe proposal fails.

The minimum dataset includes:

- redshift;
- distance measurements;
- cosmic microwave background observations;
- large-scale structure;
- primordial element abundances;
- time evolution of distant sources.

The exact statistical test must be specified before fitting.

---

## 5. Falsification Level 4 — Redshift

TTT must provide an alternative explanation for cosmological redshift.

### F4.1 Distance–Redshift Failure

If the predicted relation

\[
d_L^{TTT}(z)
\]

cannot reproduce independent luminosity-distance measurements within the pre-defined error tolerance, the model fails that test.

### F4.2 Time-Dilation Failure

If TTT predicts no appropriate time dilation while observations require it, the corresponding redshift mechanism fails.

### F4.3 Spectral Consistency Failure

If the mechanism predicts spectral behavior inconsistent with independently measured source spectra, it fails.

---

## 6. Falsification Level 5 — Cosmic Microwave Background

The CMB is a critical discriminator.

A closed-universe TTT model must explain at minimum:

- the observed near-blackbody spectrum;
- angular temperature structure;
- acoustic structure;
- polarization;
- the observed statistical properties.

If TTT cannot produce these features without importing the standard hot Big Bang mechanism unchanged, its claim to replace that framework is substantially weakened.

A future version should define a quantitative likelihood comparison:

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{TTT}}
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{standard}}.
\]

---

## 7. Falsification Level 6 — Primordial Abundances

A non-Big-Bang cosmology must explain the observed abundances of light elements.

At minimum:

- hydrogen;
- deuterium;
- helium;
- lithium.

If no TTT mechanism can reproduce the observed abundance pattern, the closed-universe cosmology is falsified or requires a major revision.

---

## 8. Falsification Level 7 — Large-Scale Structure

TTT must reproduce the observed formation and statistical distribution of cosmic structure.

A model should predict, rather than merely fit:

- matter power spectrum;
- characteristic clustering scales;
- growth history;
- gravitational-lensing observations or their TTT replacement.

Failure in these independent observables counts against the model.

---

## 9. Falsification Level 8 — Gravity

If TTT rejects fundamental gravity, it must nevertheless reproduce established gravitational observations.

The model must confront:

- orbital dynamics;
- gravitational redshift;
- light deflection;
- gravitational time dilation;
- binary-pulsar behavior;
- gravitational-wave observations;
- cosmological gravitational phenomena.

A failure in any robustly measured effect that the theory claims to explain is a direct falsification of that version.

---

## 10. Falsification Level 9 — Mass

The mass-as-confined-energy hypothesis fails if the proposed geometry cannot generate stable localized states.

A successful mathematical construction must provide:

\[
E_{\mathrm{rest}}<\infty,
\]

a stable solution, and a calculable inertial mass.

If only a verbal interpretation of \(E=mc^2\) is obtained, the physical hypothesis remains unverified.

---

## 11. Falsification Level 10 — Critical Number 125

The number

\[
125=5^3
\]

is currently a model-derived candidate.

It becomes falsifiable only after a physical observable is linked to it.

The falsification procedure is:

1. derive the observable independently;
2. calculate the TTT value;
3. define an uncertainty;
4. compare with measurement.

If the measured critical quantity lies outside the predicted interval, the \(125\) hypothesis fails.

If no observable consequence can be derived, \(125\) must remain a mathematical pattern rather than a physical claim.

---

## 12. Parameter-Fitting Rule

A major danger is post-hoc fitting.

The following procedure is **not** accepted as confirmation:

1. observe a value;
2. choose TTT parameters to reproduce it;
3. report the match as a prediction.

Instead:

\[
\boxed{
\text{calibrate parameters on dataset A}
\rightarrow
\text{predict dataset B}
}
\]

The independent dataset B is the decisive test.

---

## 13. Strong Falsification

The strongest possible result against TTT is a **unique prediction** that conflicts with observation.

For example:

\[
O_{TTT}=X\pm\sigma
\]

while

\[
O_{\mathrm{obs}}=Y\pm\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}
\]

and

\[
|X-Y|
\]

is statistically incompatible under a pre-defined model.

Such a result should be recorded as **FAIL**, not explained away by adding an unconstrained parameter.

---

## 14. Revision Rules

When a test fails, only three outcomes are permitted:

### FAIL

The hypothesis is incompatible with the result.

### REVISE

A clearly identified mathematical assumption is changed, with the previous version preserved.

### UNDECIDED

The experiment or model is insufficient to distinguish the alternatives.

The repository should never convert a failed prediction into a success by changing definitions after seeing the result.

## 16. Final Criterion

TTT-Closed-Universe should be considered scientifically successful only if it reaches the following chain:

\[
\boxed{
\text{axioms}
\rightarrow
\text{mathematics}
\rightarrow
\text{dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{numerical prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{independent observation}
}
\]

And it should be considered scientifically falsified when a central, uniquely TTT prediction is robustly contradicted by observation.

The goal is therefore not to make TTT impossible to disprove.

The goal is to make it **precisely possible to disprove**.
