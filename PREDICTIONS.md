# TTT Closed Universe — Predictions

> **Status:** Candidate predictions / tests to be quantified  
> **Important:** A hypothesis is not a successful prediction until it produces a numerical result that can be compared with independent observations.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document separates the **physical predictions** of TTT-Closed-Universe from its mathematical framework.

The key cosmological hypothesis is that the universe is a **closed dynamical system** and that phenomena normally interpreted as evidence for a Big Bang and gravitational cosmology can be explained by internal geometry, energy redistribution, and phase dynamics.

The repository should not claim that these predictions have been confirmed unless an independent dataset and a reproducible analysis support them.

---

## 2. Prediction P1 — No Initial Cosmological Singularity

### Hypothesis

The universe does not originate from a singular \(t=0\) event.

### TTT expectation

The closed system possesses a pre-existing dynamical state and can undergo transitions between phases without requiring an absolute beginning.

### Required test

Construct a cosmological solution \(a(t)\), density \(\rho(t)\), and pressure \(p(t)\) that:

1. remains finite;
2. has no singular initial boundary;
3. reproduces the observed late-time universe.

### Falsification

If every mathematically consistent TTT cosmology that reproduces observations necessarily contains a singular initial state, this version of the hypothesis fails.

---

## 3. Prediction P2 — Apparent Expansion Without Fundamental Expansion

### Hypothesis

Observed cosmological redshift may arise from internal phase/energy geometry rather than fundamental expansion of space.

### Required output

A TTT model must derive a redshift relation

\[
z = F(\text{source state},\text{observer state},\text{path})
\]

and recover the observed low-redshift relation

\[
v\approx H_0d
\]

without simply inserting the Hubble law as an assumption.

### Critical requirement

The model must also reproduce:

- distance-redshift relations;
- time dilation of distant events;
- surface-brightness behavior;
- spectral evolution where applicable.

Matching only the Hubble law is insufficient.

---

## 4. Prediction P3 — Closed Energy Budget

### Hypothesis

The total universe is an internally closed energy system.

A schematic conservation law is

\[
\frac{dE_{\mathrm{total}}}{dt}=0.
\]

### Required test

Define exactly what belongs to \(E_{\mathrm{total}}\), including:

- radiation;
- matter;
- vacuum-like energy;
- geometric energy;
- the proposed TTT confinement energy.

The theory must specify the conserved quantity in a coordinate-independent or otherwise mathematically justified form.

### Falsification

If no conserved global quantity can be defined for the proposed closed cosmology, the claim must be weakened or abandoned.

---

## 5. Prediction P4 — Gravity Is Emergent, Not Fundamental

### Hypothesis

Gravity is not a fundamental interaction but an effective consequence of the geometry/energy dynamics.

### Minimum requirement

The theory must reproduce, at appropriate limits:

\[
a\approx \frac{GM}{r^2},
\]

together with the relativistic effects that have already been measured.

The equation above should be treated as a **target**, not as a TTT assumption.

### Strong test

TTT should predict at least one regime in which its effective gravity differs quantitatively from general relativity.

If no differing prediction exists, the proposal is empirically indistinguishable from the competing theory in that regime.

---

## 6. Prediction P5 — Mass as Confined Energy

### Hypothesis

A massive particle is a stable geometrically confined energy configuration.

### Required derivation

A complete model should produce:

\[
E_{\mathrm{rest}}=mc^2
\]

as a consequence of the stable configuration, rather than merely reinterpret the equation.

It should also calculate:

- particle rest masses;
- stability conditions;
- inertial response;
- allowed excitation modes.

### Falsification

If the proposed geometry cannot produce finite localized stable solutions, the mass hypothesis fails in its current form.

## 7. Prediction P6 — Critical Geometric Number

The model contains the sequence

\[
6,\;18,\;54,\;162,\ldots
\]

and the cubic capacities

\[
8,\;27,\;64,\;125,\ldots
\]

with

\[
54\le64,\qquad162>125.
\]

This identifies \(125\) as a candidate critical capacity.

### Required test

A physical model must derive an observable consequence from this number.

For example, the theory might predict a transition scale

\[
N_c=125
\]

or a corresponding dimensionless critical parameter.

### Warning

The numerical coincidence alone is not a physical prediction.

## 8. Prediction P7 — Dynamical Equilibrium

The tetrahedral core predicts the equilibrium condition

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{4}\vec v_i=0.
\]

In a dynamical extension, a disturbance

\[
\delta\vec P\neq0
\]

should generate a restoring response.

### Required test

Define a measurable order parameter \(D(t)\) and predict its evolution, for example

\[
D(t)=|\delta\vec P(t)|.
\]

A concrete model should provide a predicted decay, oscillation, or redistribution law.

### Falsification

A controlled perturbation that systematically produces behavior incompatible with the predicted dynamics falsifies that version of the model.

## 9. Prediction P8 — Cosmological Equation of State

The current TTT framework considers a possible effective dark-energy-like component.

A future model should predict

\[
w(z)=\frac{p(z)}{\rho(z)}
\]

rather than fitting \(w\) freely.

The important question is whether TTT predicts a measurable deviation from

\[
w=-1.
\]

A nontrivial prediction could take the form

\[
w(z)=-1+\Delta w(z),
\]

where \(\Delta w(z)\) is derived from the TTT dynamics.

---

## 10. Prediction P9 — Matter / Radiation / Geometric Energy Ratios

The earlier framework considered a ratio such as

\[
68:27:5.
\]

At present, the mechanism producing this ratio is not derived.

Therefore:

> **68:27:5 is not yet a prediction.**

To become one, TTT must derive the ratio from independently defined parameters and then compare it with observations without fitting those parameters to the observed ratio.

---

## 11. Prediction P10 — Distinguishing TTT From Standard Cosmology

The strongest prediction is not that TTT can reproduce an observation.

It is that TTT produces an observation for which competing models predict a different value.

The repository therefore prioritizes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{TTT prediction}
\neq
\text{standard-model prediction}
}
\]

as the final criterion for an empirical test.

## 13. Rule for Promotion

A candidate becomes a **TTT prediction** only when all of the following exist:

1. explicit equations;
2. defined parameters;
3. parameter values determined independently of the target observation;
4. numerical prediction;
5. specified dataset or experiment;
6. reproducible analysis;
7. pre-defined pass/fail criterion.

Until then it remains a hypothesis or research target.
